Split, sing, score.

Audio tools for vocals. Everything runs on this machine; your audio is never uploaded anywhere else. Every result is kept in the Library at the bottom.

Separate

Split

Takes a song apart: the singing on its own, and the music on its own.

Drop a song (mp3, wav, m4a, flac…). Tick four parts to also separate the drums and bass.

Change the voice

Convert

A voice costume. You sing something, give it a short sample of someone else's voice, and it sounds like they sang it. Your tune and timing stay the same.

Drop your singing as the performance, and 1–30 s of the other voice as the target voice. Both work best as clean vocals with no music, so split songs first.

Performance
Target voice

Slow without an NVIDIA GPU: on an M2 Mac a 30-second clip took about 10 minutes at 30 steps. Fewer steps is faster and rougher.

Line up

Sync

Lines your singing up with the real song so it lands on the beat, then plays your voice over the song's music.

Drop the original song, with the singer's vocals in it, and a recording of you singing it. It matches your melody against the original singer's, so a music-only track won't work. Your recording is best a cappella; if you sang over music, tick the box below. It works in any language, in a different key, or if you only sang part of the song. Each line is moved into place on its own; your singing is never sped up or slowed down.

Pitch match

A score card for your singing: how often you hit the right notes, whether you sing too high or too low, and a chart of your notes next to the singer's. It doesn't change your recording.

Drop the original song, with the singer's vocals in it (the full song or the vocals alone), and a recording of you singing it. You're scored against the original singer's vocal, so a music-only track won't work. Singing in a different key isn't counted against you.

Correct

Tune to original

Auto-tune aimed at the real singer's notes. It nudges your off notes toward theirs, and it still sounds like you.

Drop the original song, with the singer's vocals in it, and a recording of you singing it. Your notes are nudged toward the original singer's vocal, so a music-only track won't work. Strength sets how hard it nudges. Your timing doesn't change, and you stay in your own key.

Original song (with vocals)
Your singing

100% copies the original's pitch exactly, including its vibrato and slides, and sounds the most processed. Around 60–80% keeps more of your own expression.

Transpose

Change key

Makes a whole song higher or lower without speeding it up or slowing it down, so it fits your voice when you sing along.

Drop a song and choose how many semitones to move it. Negative numbers make it lower.

Song

Not sure how far to move it? Pitch match tells you how many semitones above or below the original you naturally sang.

Time the words

Lyrics timing

Makes karaoke lyrics: works out when each word is sung, so the words can light up in time.

Drop a song. You get LRC files (for karaoke players) and SRT files (for video subtitles). If you add lyrics, put each sung line on its own line, pressing Enter between lines: each one becomes a separate line on screen.

Song

Look up lyrics sends only the artist and title (never your audio) to LRCLIB, a free, open lyrics database. Check what it finds: it may be a different version of the song. Without lyrics it uses what Whisper hears, which on singing is often wrong. With English lyrics pasted, each word is pinned precisely in clips up to about 40 s; in longer songs some words can be a few seconds off.

Write down the words

Transcribe

Listens to a video and writes down what's said or sung, so you have the words as text and as subtitles.

Drop a video (mp4, mov…) or an audio file. Any language. You get the transcript as a text file and as SRT subtitles you can load into a video editor or YouTube.

Video or audio

Uses Whisper on this machine. Speech comes out well; singing is harder, and some words may be wrong, so check the text.
