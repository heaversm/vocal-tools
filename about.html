About Vocal Tools

These tools were assembled from various audio tools and libraries in order to build the Karaoke Captcha prototype. I figured I'd make them accessible to others for their own purposes.

Everything runs on your own machine. No accounts, no API keys, nothing uploaded.

Get started

Install and run

Install

You need a Mac with Apple Silicon (Linux may work, untested), Python 3.10 or 3.11, ffmpeg, and about 4.5 GB of disk. Clone the repo and follow the instructions on GitHub:

git clone https://github.com/heaversm/vocal-tools.git
cd vocal-tools
./setup.sh

Run

On a Mac, open Vocal Tools from ~/Applications (setup builds it; drag it to the Dock to keep it handy). Or double-click start.command in Finder, or run ./start.command. It opens http://localhost:8010 in your browser.

The tools run quietly in the background: they start when you log in and restart themselves if they crash. To stop them, run ./service.sh stop.

Under the hood

What it uses

Split. Drop a song, get the vocals and the instrumental as separate files, or four parts: vocals, drums, bass and everything else. Uses Demucs, a state-of-the-art music source separation model that runs on your machine.

How: Demucs v4 (the htdemucs model) is a Hybrid Transformer. It looks at the sound two ways at once, as a waveform and as a spectrogram (a picture of which frequencies are playing when), and a Transformer in the middle lets the two views inform each other.

Convert. Drop a performance (the melody, timing and delivery to keep) and a target voice (1–30 s of the voice it should sound like). You get the performance back in the target voice. Uses Seed-VC, a zero-shot voice conversion model that runs on your machine. Works for singing and speech.

How: "zero-shot" means it needs no training on the target voice, just the short sample. A small patch included in the repo lets it run on Apple's GPUs.

Sync. Drop a song and a recording of you singing it. Your singing is lined up with the song and mixed over the instrumental. It matches by melody rather than lyrics, so it works in any language, in a different key or octave, and for a take that covers only part of the song.

How: Demucs pulls the singer's vocal out of the song. Both vocals are turned into a sequence of which of the 12 notes is sounding, with librosa, so octave doesn't matter. Dynamic time warping, a standard way to match two sequences that run at different speeds, finds where your take sits in the song. Each phrase is then placed on its own, without stretching your timing.

Pitch match. Same inputs as Sync. After lining your take up, it tracks the pitch of your voice and the original singer's and scores how often you were on the note, how far you typically missed, and whether you lean sharp or flat. A chart shows your pitch over the original melody.

How: pitch is tracked every 23 ms with librosa's pYIN pitch tracker. "On the note" means within 50 cents (half a semitone), measured against the melody moved into your key and octave.

Tune to original. Same inputs again. It pulls your pitch toward the original singer's notes, moment by moment, with a strength control, keeping your voice and timing. Re-synthesis uses the WORLD vocoder, a fast and high-quality vocoder which parameterizes speech into three components:

  • f0: pitch contour
  • sp: harmonic spectral envelope
  • ap: aperiodic spectral envelope (relative to the harmonic spectral envelope)

How: only f0, the pitch, is changed. The other two carry the character of your voice, so it still sounds like you.

Change key. Drop a song and choose how many semitones to move it, up or down. The speed stays the same. You can also get the instrumental in the new key, for singing along. Uses Rubber Band, a software library for audio time-stretching and pitch-shifting. It lets you change the tempo and pitch of a recording independently of one another. It's used here through Spotify's pedalboard audio tools suite.

Lyrics timing. Drop a song, optionally paste its lyrics, and get them timed word by word as LRC (line and word level, for karaoke players) and SRT (subtitles). Uses faster-whisper, a reimplementation of OpenAI's Whisper model using CTranslate2 (up to 4 times faster than OpenAI's version), plus forced alignment for English when you paste lyrics.

How: Whisper's own word timings on singing can be seconds off. When you paste the lyrics, a speech model (wav2vec 2.0) scores, moment by moment, how likely each letter is. Forced alignment then finds the one path through those scores that spells your lyrics in order, which gives every word a start and end time.
